0The gap that organises everything

Every theorem below serves one decomposition. URB reports how far a learned fleet sits from the best achievable cost; that distance splits into two terms with different owners.

\[ \underbrace{C(s_{\text{learned}}) - C(s^{\mathrm{SO}})}_{\text{what URB measures}} \;=\; \underbrace{C(s_{\text{learned}}) - C(s^{\mathrm{NE}})}_{\text{learning gap}} \;+\; \underbrace{C(s^{\mathrm{NE}}) - C(s^{\mathrm{SO}})}_{\text{equilibrium gap}} \]

Here \(C\) is total CAV travel time with the human population fixed, \(s^{\mathrm{SO}}\) the social optimum and \(s^{\mathrm{NE}}\) the equilibrium that best-response dynamics reach from the learned profile. Three consequences carry the paper:

  • Under selfish rewards, no selfishly-optimising learner closes the equilibrium gap (B4). PACT‑1's defensible claim is shrinking the learning gap — reaching and tracking equilibrium where blind learners do not — especially under drift.
  • At σ = 0 the equilibrium gap on this city is small (A7). That is the theoretical reason AON and greedy (3.01) beat every MARL method: a load-oblivious shortest path is near-optimal in light traffic.
  • The severity dial divides capacity, pushing the instance out of light traffic so that both gaps become non-trivial (A4, A7). This is the formal justification for injecting non-stationarity at all.
Corrections to the earlier outline
  1. Frozen humans are fixed background load, not Stackelberg followers. With should_humans_adapt: false (config1) humans never respond, so the CAV game is a congestion game with constant offsets. Stackelberg routing applies only when humans adapt after mutation.
  2. The 4/3 bound is the wrong anchor. Roughgarden–Tardos is nonatomic and additive. URB has 222 discrete vehicles, where the affine bound is 5/2 for pure equilibria (A2); and the severity layer's harm takes a maximum over links, which is a bottleneck game — a different class (A6).
  3. An uninformative estimator gets zero reward-gradient on trust, not a negative one. Trust decays only through the entropy bonus (D3). And with bias 2.2 and EMA 0.9 the trust gradient is 27.8× smaller than an unbiased sigmoid's (D4), so "learned" trust may be indistinguishable from fixed.

1Formal model

The codebase contains two distinct cost layers, and a theorem must say which one it is about. Conflating them is the fastest way to state something false.

Primitives

Agents \(N\), \(|N| = n\); controllable \(M \subseteq N\), fixed \(H = N \setminus M\). Each agent chooses one of \(K\) options (routes) \(s_i \in \mathcal{K}_i\). Elements (links) \(e \in E\) with capacity \(c_e \gt 0\). Co-presence \(O \in \{0,1\}^{n\times n}\) is symmetric (interval overlap) with \(O_{ii} = 1\); write \(O' = O - I\). A profile is \(s = (s_1,\dots,s_n)\), with \(s_H\) held fixed throughout.

Layer N — native coupling, additive (what PACT‑1 models)

\[ \tau_i(s) \;=\; \sum_{e \in s_i} \Big( b_e + a_e \sum_{j \ne i} O_{ij}\, \mathbf{1}[e \in s_j] \Big), \qquad a_e, b_e \ge 0 \]

\(b_e\) is free-flow time on \(e\); \(a_e\) the delay one co-present vehicle adds. PACT‑1's basis \(G_m[c,c'] = \sum_{e \in c \cap c' \cap m} w_e / L_c\) with \(w_e = \text{length}_e/\text{lanes}_e\) maps onto this with \(a_e = \beta^*_{m(e)} w_e / \bar v\) and \(b_e = (1+\beta^*_0)\,\mathrm{fft}_e\), under the constant-speed approximation \(\mathrm{fft}_c / L_c \approx 1/\bar v\).

Layer H — injected harm, bottleneck (what the severity dial does)

\[ u_i(s;g) = \max_{e \in s_i} \frac{\sum_{j\ne i} O_{ij}\mathbf{1}[e\in s_j]}{T_i\, c_e\, g_e}, \qquad m_i(s;g) = \frac{1 + \alpha\, u_i(s;g)}{1 + \alpha\, u_i(s;1)}, \qquad t_i = m_i(s;g)\, t_i^{\mathrm{sim}}(s) \]

with \(\alpha = 2.28\), \(\beta = 1\), and \(g_e(t,\sigma) = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(1 - \sigma L A(t) s_e,\,[10^{-3},1]\big)\), \(L = 0.14\).

The steered policy and the estimator

\[ \tilde\pi(k \mid x) = \operatorname{softmax}\!\big(\ell(x) - g\,\kappa\, z\big)_k, \quad z_k = \frac{\hat t_k - \bar{\hat t}}{\operatorname{sd}(\hat t)}, \quad \hat t_k = \mathrm{fft}_k\big(1 + \hat\beta^\top \psi_k\big) \]
\[ g = \mathrm{conf}\cdot\big(\eta\, g_{\text{prev}} + (1-\eta)\, g_{\max}\,\sigma(w + b)\big), \qquad \eta = 0.9,\; b = 2.2,\; g_{\max} = \kappa = 1 \]

\(\hat\beta\) is per-agent RLS with forgetting \(\mu = 0.999\) and prior \(p_0 = 10\); the confidence gate averages prediction confidence over the agent's \(K\) candidate regressors. Social cost is \(C(s) = \sum_{i \in M} \tau_i(s)\) (Layer N) or \(C_H(s) = \sum_{i\in M} t_i(s)\) (with harm).

Two declared operators, not one. urb_ns/network.py builds \(W\) from incidence over capacity (lanes × 600 veh/h), averaged over agent \(i\)'s links. pact1/basis.py builds \(G_m\) from shared per-lane length by road class, normalised by \(i\)'s own route length. Both scale as \(1/\text{lanes}\); \(G_m\) adds length. Part A is stated in Layer N's coefficients \(a_e\) so it can be mapped onto either — but the mapping must be written down.

Part A

Equilibrium structure — making the ceiling a theorem

The committed decomposition is an accounting identity over load. Part A turns it into statements about what equilibria and learners can achieve.

PropositionA1 Exact potential on the co-presence graph proved here

Under Layer N with symmetric \(O\), \(a_e, b_e \ge 0\) and \(s_H\) fixed, the CAV routing game admits the exact potential

\[ \Phi(s) = \sum_{e \in E}\Big( b_e\, n_e(s) + a_e \sum_{i \lt j} O_{ij}\,\mathbf{1}[e\in s_i]\,\mathbf{1}[e \in s_j] \Big). \]

Hence pure Nash equilibria exist, every better-response sequence is finite, and independent best-responding CAVs converge.

In words: the native URB coupling is a graphical congestion game. Equilibria exist and simple dynamics find them — so when MARL fails on URB, it is not because there is nothing to converge to.

Proof. Let CAV \(i\) deviate from \(s_i\) to \(s_i'\). Terms of \(\Phi\) not involving \(i\) are unchanged, and human counts are constant. Using \(O_{ij} = O_{ji}\),

\[ \Phi(s_i', s_{-i}) - \Phi(s) = \sum_{e\in s_i'}\Big(b_e + a_e\sum_{j\ne i} O_{ij}\mathbf{1}[e\in s_j]\Big) - \sum_{e\in s_i}\Big(b_e + a_e\sum_{j\ne i}O_{ij}\mathbf{1}[e\in s_j]\Big) = \tau_i(s_i',s_{-i}) - \tau_i(s). \]

So \(\Phi\) is an exact potential (Rosenthal; Monderer–Shapley). Its minimiser over the finite profile space is a pure equilibrium, and each strict improvement lowers \(\Phi\), so improvement paths are finite. ∎

Scope

This holds in travel-time units, which is what selfish agents are paid in. In the regression target's relative-excess units every cost is divided by the agent's own \(\mathrm{fft}_{s_i}\), a choice-dependent factor, and the potential property is lost. The link to PACT‑1's basis also rests on the constant-speed approximation.

ObligatesNothing extra; it is the foundation for A2, A3, A6 and A8.

TheoremA2 Smoothness, and why it reaches learners classical (i)strategy (ii)

A cost-minimisation game is \((\lambda,\mu)\)-smooth if for all profiles \(s, s^*\)

\[ \sum_{i} \tau_i(s_i^*, s_{-i}) \;\le\; \lambda\, C(s^*) + \mu\, C(s), \qquad \mu \lt 1, \]

which bounds the price of anarchy by \(\lambda/(1-\mu)\) for pure, mixed, correlated and coarse correlated equilibria — and therefore for the time-averaged outcome of any no-regret dynamics.

(i) If every pair is co-present (\(O \equiv \mathbf{1}\)) and \(b_e \ge a_e\), Layer N is an atomic affine congestion game with latency \((b_e - a_e) + a_e k\); it is \((5/3, 1/3)\)-smooth, so PoA ≤ 5/2.

(ii) For general symmetric \(O\): find \((\lambda,\mu)\), which will necessarily depend on \(\max_e a_e/b_e\).

In words: the ceiling becomes a bound that applies to whatever no-regret learners converge to on average — which is what connects equilibrium theory to MARL, not just to idealised equilibria.

Strategy for (ii). Per element, with \(y = \mathbf{1}[e\in s^*]\) and \(z = \mathbf{1}[e\in s]\), the requirement is

\[ b_e|y| + a_e\, y^\top O' z \;\le\; \lambda\big(b_e|y| + a_e\, y^\top O' y\big) + \mu\big(b_e|z| + a_e\, z^\top O' z\big). \]

Two difficulties. First, \(O'\) has zero diagonal and zero trace, so it is indefinite and the Cauchy–Schwarz route is closed. Second, self-exclusion forces a load-dependent constant: a single player on \(e\) under \(s^*\), facing one co-present player under \(s\), gives \(b_e + a_e \le (\lambda+\mu)b_e\), i.e. \(a_e \le (\lambda+\mu-1)\,b_e\). The template is the Christodoulou–Koutsoupias integer inequality \(y(z+1) \le \tfrac53 y^2 + \tfrac13 z^2\); graphical congestion games are the nearest known class.

ObligatesDeclared-model PoA on saint_arnoult — equilibrium by best response (A1 guarantees convergence), optimum by local search.

CorollaryA3 The bound is tight for the class proved here

No PoA bound below 5/2 holds for Layer N with \(b_e \ge a_e\).

Proof. The class contains \(O \equiv \mathbf{1}\) with \(b_e = a_e\), which is exactly the pure-linear latency \(a_e k\). The classical lower-bound instances for atomic linear congestion games live there and attain 5/2. ∎

Pair with A2 in the paper: "the gap is tight" is a much stronger claim than either direction alone.

PropositionA4 Severity scales the stakes, not the structure proved herechecked

For uniform derating \(g_e \equiv g\) and any contributor subset \(S\),

\[ u_i^S(s;g) = \frac{u_i^S(s;1)}{g}, \qquad \Delta_i^S = u_i^S(s;1)\Big(\frac1g - 1\Big). \]

Every ceiling share \(\Delta^S/\Delta^{S'}\) is therefore independent of σ, and total excess scales as \(\mathbb{E}[1/g - 1]\). With heterogeneous sensitivity \(s_e\), shares move only when the binding element switches.

Proof. A constant \(g\) scales every candidate element's ratio equally, so the argmax in \(u_i\) is unchanged and \(1/g\) factors out of the maximum. The same holds for each subset's loading and for the own-vehicle term. ∎

Checked against the certificate

mean over wet days of (1/g − 1): σ=1 0.07993 · σ=2 0.18215 · σ=3 0.31945

excess ratio σ2/σ1: predicted 2.279 · certificate 2.330

The uniform law explains nearly all of the severity scaling; the extra 2% comes from high-sensitivity links, where \(1/(1-x)\) is more convex. On saint_arnoult binding links rarely switch.

For the paper this cuts both ways. It is a clean, confirmed prediction — and it says the dial does not reshape who causes the damage on this city. Any claim that severity changes the coordination structure needs heterogeneous sensitivity to bite.

PropositionA5 Exchangeable scaling law for the coordination gap proved herechecked

If the controllable set is a uniformly random subset of size \(m\) and every peer contributes mean coupling \(q\) in own-vehicle units, then

\[ (\text{fixed},\ \text{own},\ \text{peer}) \;=\; \frac{\big((n-m)\,q,\ \ 1,\ \ (m-1)\,q\big)}{(n-1)\,q + 1}. \]

The coordination gap is affine in \(m\), and irreducible share vanishes at \(m = n\).

Proof. A controllable agent has \(m-1\) controllable peers and \(n-m\) fixed ones. Under exchangeability each contributes \(q\) in expectation, its own vehicle contributes 1, and excess is proportional to load. Normalise. ∎

The human coupling matches the calibrated \(q\) almost exactly (0.01939 vs 0.01948), so the entire miss is controllable agents loading each other 45% more than exchangeability predicts. Timing does not explain it (1.03); shared routes explain part (1.16). The rest is not accounted for by the mean-over-links operator — the binding-link maximum and the renormalisation step are the remaining candidates.

Consequence for experiments

URB makes the first 40% of agents by id controllable, and that prefix is spatially clustered. An N-scaling experiment run that way conflates how many agents are controllable with which ones. Randomise the controllable set, or report the selection rule and its coupling ratio.

Also state in the paper that the decomposition is an exact partition only when the binding element is the same for every contributor subset; otherwise the code renormalises (scale = tot / s) and the shares are a proportional attribution.

PropositionA6 Transferring the bound through the harm layer proved here

Suppose simulator time equals Layer N cost and Layer N is \((\lambda,\mu)\)-smooth. Since \(1 \le m_i \le 1/g_{\min}\),

\[ \tau_i(s) \le t_i(s) \le \tau_i(s)/g_{\min} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathrm{PoA}_H \;\le\; \frac{\lambda}{g_{\min}\,(g_{\min} - \mu)} \qquad \text{whenever } g_{\min} \gt \mu . \]

Proof. For \(g \le 1\) and \(x \ge 0\), \((1 + x/g)/(1+x) \le 1/g\), which gives the sandwich. An equilibrium \(s\) of the harmed game satisfies \(\tau_i(s) \le t_i(s) \le t_i(s_i^*, s_{-i}) \le \tau_i(s_i^*, s_{-i})/g\), so it is a \(\gamma\)-approximate equilibrium of Layer N with \(\gamma = 1/g_{\min}\). Summing and applying smoothness, \(C(s)(1-\gamma\mu) \le \gamma\lambda\, C(s^*)\). Finally \(C_H(s) \le C(s)/g_{\min}\) and \(C_H(\mathrm{OPT}_H) \ge C(\mathrm{OPT}_N)\). ∎

Values with (λ, μ) = (5/3, 1/3)

g_min = 1.00 → 2.50 · 0.86 (σ=1 peak) → 3.68 · 0.58 (σ=3 peak, uniform) → 11.65

g_min = 0.16 (σ=3, sensitivity 2.0) → bound does not apply (g_min ≤ 1/3)

Honest reading: the transfer is valid but loose, and it breaks exactly in the regime the σ = 3 runs occupy on high-sensitivity links. The combined game is neither purely additive nor purely bottleneck. Bottleneck congestion games admit pure strong equilibria (Harks, Hoefer, Klimm, Skopalik) but can have unbounded PoA in general routing (Banner–Orda); whether this instance class is bounded is open.

ConjectureA7 Light traffic, and why severity restores coordination value classical frameworkconjecture for URB

For affine latencies \(b_e + a_e x\) with congestible ratio \(\rho = \max_e a_e d_e / b_e\), the Pigou-bound specialisation of the anarchy value gives, in the nonatomic approximation,

\[ \mathrm{PoA} \;\le\; \frac{1+\rho}{1 + \tfrac34\rho}, \qquad \rho \to \frac{\rho}{g} \text{ under derating}. \]

Conjecture: the atomic URB instance satisfies a bound of the same form, so its equilibrium gap is small at σ = 0 and grows with σ.

Derivation. On a Pigou network, route demand \(r\) either all on \(\ell(x) = b + ax\) or split against a constant link at \(\ell(r)\). The optimal split puts \(r/2\) on \(\ell\), costing \(rb + \tfrac34 a r^2\); equilibrium costs \(r(b + ar)\). The ratio is \((1+\rho)/(1+\tfrac34\rho)\) with \(\rho = ar/b\), increasing in \(\rho\), with limit 4/3. Derating multiplies the congestible term by \(1/g\) and leaves free-flow time alone. ∎

Illustration at the calibrated mean load α·u ≈ 0.50

σ=0: ρ 0.50 → ≤ 1.091 · σ=1 peak: 0.58 → ≤ 1.101 · σ=3 peak: 0.86 → ≤ 1.131

σ=3, sensitivity 2.0: 3.13 → ≤ 1.234 · limit → 1.333

These use the mean; the bound needs the maximum over elements, which will be larger. Treat the column as a shape, not a result.

This is the most decision-relevant statement in the document. It explains why load-oblivious shortest paths win on URB today, and it predicts that the dial is what gives coordination something to do. If the computed curve stays flat near 1 at σ = 3, the injected non-stationarity does not create coordination value on this city — and that is worth knowing before spending the sweep budget.

PropositionA8 Separating the learning gap from the equilibrium gap proved here

Define ex-post regret \(r_i(s) = \tau_i(s) - \min_{k}\tau_i(k, s_{-i}) \ge 0\). Then \(r_i(s) = 0\) for all \(i\) iff \(s\) is an equilibrium, and for the best-response limit \(s^{\mathrm{BR}}\) reached from \(s\),

\[ \Phi(s) - \Phi(s^{\mathrm{BR}}) \;\ge\; \max_{i \in M} r_i(s). \]

Proof. Let \(i^\star\) attain the maximum regret. Its best response lowers \(\Phi\) by exactly \(r_{i^\star}(s)\) (A1), and every later improving step lowers \(\Phi\) further. ∎

This is the instrument that makes the paper's claim measurable. Regret is computable at test time from the declared model and \(\hat\beta\), with no re-simulation — trustworthy to the extent fit_gain is high (not raw fit_r2, which the per-agent intercept inflates). Low regret with high cost means the method reached a bad equilibrium; high regret means it never reached one. Under selfish rewards PACT‑1 can only move the first term.

ObligatesReport mean and max ex-post regret per arm on the 100 test days.

Part B

Performativity and trust — the informational Braess result

The estimator's predictions move the fleet, which moves the load, which is what the estimator predicts. Part B is where the paper's most novel theory lives.

PropositionB1 The steered fleet is a z-normalised logit QRE proved here

Assume uninformative host logits, a nonatomic fleet, and exact predictions at the induced profile. Then any stationary choice distribution satisfies

\[ p = \operatorname{softmax}\!\big(-g\kappa\, z(c(p))\big), \qquad z(c) = \frac{c - \bar c}{\operatorname{sd}(c)}, \]

a logit quantal response equilibrium with effective rationality \(g\kappa/\operatorname{sd}(c(p))\). A fixed point exists whenever \(\operatorname{sd}(c(p)) \ge s_{\min} \gt 0\) on the simplex. At \(g = 0\) the split is uniform; as \(g\) grows it approaches the equilibrium support.

Proof. On the region where the spread of costs is bounded below, the map is a composition of continuous functions from the simplex to itself; Brouwer gives a fixed point. ∎

Where existence fails

When all predicted costs are equal the implementation defines the shift as zero rather than NaN (the floor property requires it). That makes the map discontinuous exactly at ties, and B3 shows this is where fixed points genuinely disappear. The z-normalisation that makes κ dimensionless is also what breaks global Lipschitz continuity — which matters again in B2.

TheoremB2 Performative stability of the estimator proof strategy

Let \(\theta = \hat\beta\), let \(\mathcal{D}(\theta)\) be the distribution of \((\psi, y)\) produced by steering with \(\theta\), and let the loss be \((y - \psi^\top\theta)^2\). Assume

  • strong convexity: \(2\lambda_{\min}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}(\theta)}\psi\psi^\top\big) \ge \gamma \gt 0\) on the region of interest;
  • joint smoothness \(\beta_J\) on bounded support (the sensor clip and finite peer counts provide it);
  • ε-sensitivity: \(W_1(\mathcal{D}(\theta), \mathcal{D}(\theta')) \le \varepsilon\,\lVert\theta - \theta'\rVert\).

Then repeated risk minimisation converges linearly to a unique performatively stable \(\theta_{\mathrm{PS}}\) if \(\varepsilon \lt \gamma/\beta_J\). To prove: the sensitivity is linear in trust,

\[ \varepsilon \;\le\; g\,\kappa\, L_z\, L_{\mathrm{sm}}\, L_W \quad\Longrightarrow\quad g_{\mathrm{stab}} = \frac{\gamma}{\beta_J\,\kappa\, L_z\, L_{\mathrm{sm}}\, L_W}. \]

Strategy. Chain four Lipschitz maps: \(\theta \mapsto z(\hat t(\theta))\) with constant \(L_z\), finite only where \(\operatorname{sd}(\hat t) \ge s_{\min}\); the logit shift to the choice distribution, \(L_{\mathrm{sm}}\); one agent's changed choice to its peers' regressors through the declared operator, \(L_W\) (bounded by the largest row of \(G_m\) times co-presence degree); and choices to the joint law of \((\psi, y)\). The base result is Perdomo, Zrnic, Mendler-Dünner and Hardt (2020). RLS with μ = 0.999 approximates repeated minimisation over a window of \(1/(1-\mu) = 1000\) days; the stochastic variant (Mendler-Dünner et al. 2020) and the multi-learner version — each CAV has its own \(\theta_i\), a decision-dependent game (Narang et al.) — should carry the same threshold with a spectral condition. Verify both before citing.

In words: past a critical trust, the estimator is chasing data its own steering keeps changing. The observable signature is pred_gain falling while fit_gain holds — the physics is right but the forecast no longer converges.

ObligatesPlot pred_gain − fit_gain against applied trust across the fixed-trust sweep.

TheoremB3 Two options: sign steering, an exact optimum, and flapping proved (i–iv)conjecture (v)

Take \(K = 2\), a nonatomic unit mass of CAVs, \(c_1(p) = t_1 + ap\), \(c_2(p) = t_2 + a(1-p)\) with \(\Delta = t_2 - t_1 \in (0, 2a)\), uninformative host logits and exact predictions.

(i) For any \(c_1 \ne c_2\), \(z = \pm 1\): z-score steering is sign steering, and the steered split toward the cheaper option is \(p = \sigma(2g\kappa)\), whatever the size of the cost difference.

(ii) \(p^{\mathrm{SO}} = \tfrac12 + \tfrac{\Delta}{4a}\) and \(p^{\mathrm{UE}} = \tfrac12 + \tfrac{\Delta}{2a}\), so \(\tfrac12 \lt p^{\mathrm{SO}} \lt p^{\mathrm{UE}}\).

(iii) A stationary split exists iff \(\sigma(2g\kappa) \le p^{\mathrm{UE}}\). On that range social cost falls on \([0, g^*]\) and rises on \([g^*, g_{\mathrm{UE}}]\), with

\[ g^* = \frac{\operatorname{logit}(p^{\mathrm{SO}})}{2\kappa} \quad\text{attaining the social optimum exactly.} \]

(iv) If \(\sigma(2g\kappa) \gt p^{\mathrm{UE}}\), no stationary split exists; with one-step-lagged predictions the fleet alternates between \(\sigma(2g\kappa)\) and \(1 - \sigma(2g\kappa)\).

(v) With exponentially-smoothed predictions (weight ρ), the fleet chatters around \(p^{\mathrm{UE}}\) and its time-average converges to the selfish equilibrium.

Proof. (i) Two values have population standard deviation \(|c_1 - c_2|/2\), so each z-score is \(\pm1\); the logit gap is \(2g\kappa\). (ii) First-order condition of \(S(p) = pt_1 + (1-p)t_2 + a(p^2 + (1-p)^2)\), and \(c_1 = c_2\). (iii) \(S\) is a convex quadratic minimised at \(p^{\mathrm{SO}}\), and \(g \mapsto \sigma(2g\kappa)\) is increasing; stationarity requires the sign to stay consistent, \(c_1(p) \le c_2(p) \iff p \le p^{\mathrm{UE}}\). (iv) The only other candidate, \(1 - \sigma(2g\kappa) \lt \tfrac12 \lt p^{\mathrm{UE}}\), contradicts its own sign requirement; at an exact tie the shift is zero, giving \(p = \tfrac12 \ne p^{\mathrm{UE}}\). ∎

TheoremB4 Selfish trust reproduces selfish routing proved for K = 2

In the setting of B3, let each CAV choose its own trust \(g_i \in [0, g_{\max}]\) to minimise its own expected cost. Then:

(i) whenever \(c_1(\bar p) \lt c_2(\bar p)\), every agent's best response is \(g_i = g_{\max}\);

(ii) symmetric trust equilibria have \(\bar p = \min\big(\sigma(2g_{\max}\kappa),\ p^{\mathrm{UE}}\big)\), the second case through the chattering of B3(v);

(iii) if \(\sigma(2g_{\max}\kappa) \ge p^{\mathrm{SO}}\), the trust equilibrium is inefficient, with \(S(\bar p)/S(p^{\mathrm{SO}}) \le S(p^{\mathrm{UE}})/S(p^{\mathrm{SO}}) \le 4/3\).

Proof. Agent \(i\)'s cost is \(p_i c_1(\bar p) + (1-p_i)c_2(\bar p)\) with \(p_i = \sigma(2g_i\kappa)\); a single nonatomic agent does not move \(\bar p\), so \(\partial J_i/\partial g_i = 2\kappa\,\sigma'(2g_i\kappa)\,(c_1 - c_2) \lt 0\). Then \(\bar p \in [p^{\mathrm{SO}}, p^{\mathrm{UE}}]\) where the convex \(S\) is increasing, and the last ratio is the nonatomic affine price of anarchy. ∎

Corollary B4.1 — what the paper may claim

Under selfish rewards the equilibrium gap is not closable by any selfishly-optimising learner, PACT‑1 included. A claim to close the coordination gap requires a social objective.

The severity layer currently refuses every behaviour except selfish (_LINEAR_REWARD_BEHAVIOURS = {"selfish"}), while URB ships social, collaborative and altruistic task configs. Extending the layer's reward mapping to one of those is a precondition for any coordination-gap claim.

This is sobering and it is also clarifying. It is the formal version of the compensation commons: trust is individually rational and collectively excessive, exactly as route choice is.

Part C

Identification in a converging population

The fleet needs variety to identify the coupling, and learning destroys that variety. Part C turns the measured pathologies into theorems.

TheoremC1 The tracking floor classical

For \(y_t = \psi_t^\top\theta_t + e_t\) with noise variance \(\sigma_e^2\), random-walk drift of variance \(\sigma_w^2\) and persistently exciting \(\psi_t\), exponentially-weighted RLS has steady-state tracking error

\[ \mathrm{MSE}(\mu) \approx c_1 (1-\mu)\,\sigma_e^2 + c_2\,\frac{\sigma_w^2}{1-\mu}, \qquad 1-\mu^* \propto \frac{\sigma_w}{\sigma_e}, \qquad \mathrm{RMS}^* \propto \sqrt{\sigma_e\,\sigma_w}. \]

Cite the adaptive-filtering tracking analysis (e.g. Guo and Ljung, 1995) and verify the exact form. Its role in the paper is to justify declaring μ and sweeping it: the floor cannot be tuned away, only balanced.

PropositionC2 Trace confidence collapses without excitation proved herechecked, order of magnitude

If \(\psi_t \in S\) with \(\dim S \lt p\) for all \(t \ge t_0\), then for any unit \(v \perp S\)

\[ v^\top P_t^{-1} v = \mu^{\,t-t_0}\, v^\top P_{t_0}^{-1} v, \qquad \operatorname{tr} P_t \;\ge\; \frac{\mu^{-(t-t_0)}}{v^\top P_{t_0}^{-1} v} \to \infty, \]

so \(\mathrm{conf}_{\mathrm{tr}} = 1/(1 + \operatorname{tr}P_t/p_0) \to 0\) at rate \(\mu^{t}\), independent of prediction accuracy.

Proof. The information matrix obeys \(P_t^{-1} = \mu P_{t-1}^{-1} + \psi_t\psi_t^\top\), and \(v^\top\psi_t = 0\) removes the update in direction \(v\). For unit \(v\), \(v^\top P^{-1} v \ge \lambda_{\min}(P^{-1}) = 1/\lambda_{\max}(P)\), and \(\operatorname{tr}P \ge \lambda_{\max}(P)\). ∎

PropositionC3 The averaged prediction gate floors at 1/K proved herechecked in simulation

Suppose from \(t_0\) the chosen regressor is constant, \(\psi_c\), and every other candidate \(\psi_k\) has a component orthogonal to it. Then

\[ \psi_c^\top P_t\,\psi_c \to 1-\mu, \qquad \psi_k^\top P_t\,\psi_k \to \infty, \qquad \frac1K\sum_{k} \mathrm{conf}_{\mathrm{pred}}(\psi_k) \;\to\; \frac1K\Big(1 + O(1-\mu)\Big). \]

Proof. The information matrix tends to \(\psi_c\psi_c^\top/(1-\mu) + \varepsilon_t I\) with \(\varepsilon_t \to 0\). Sherman–Morrison gives \(\psi_c^\top P\psi_c = \lVert\psi_c\rVert^2/(\varepsilon_t + \lVert\psi_c\rVert^2/(1-\mu)) \to 1-\mu\), so the chosen candidate's confidence tends to \(1/(1 + p(1-\mu)/(p_0\lVert\psi_c\rVert^2)) \approx 1\). Orthogonal components see variance \(\approx 1/\varepsilon_t \to \infty\), so the other candidates' confidence tends to 0. ∎

LemmaC4 Excitation is bounded by fleet indecision proved here

Let \(\psi\) be agent \(i\)'s executed regressor, whose range has diameter \(D\). Then

\[ \operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(\psi) \;\le\; D^2\Big[\big(1 - \max_k \pi_i(k)\big) + \sum_{j\ne i} O_{ij}\big(1 - \max_k \pi_j(k)\big)\Big] \;\le\; D^2\Big[H(\pi_i) + \sum_{j \ne i} O_{ij}\, H(\pi_j)\Big]. \]

Proof. \(\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(\psi) \le \mathbb{E}\lVert\psi - \psi^\circ\rVert^2\) for the regressor \(\psi^\circ\) at the modal joint profile. The regressor depends only on \(i\)'s route and its co-present peers' routes, so it differs from \(\psi^\circ\) only if one of them leaves its mode; a union bound gives the bracket, and \(\lVert\psi - \psi^\circ\rVert \le D\). Finally \(1 - x \le -\log x\), so \(1 - \max_k \pi(k) \le H(\pi)\). No independence is needed. ∎

In words: as a population's policies harden, the data it generates loses the variety needed to identify how it is coupled. Identification and convergence are in direct tension — Feldbaum's dual-control problem, now with a population instead of a single controller.

\[ \operatorname{cond} = \frac{\big(T + \sqrt{T^2 - 4s^2}\big)^2}{4s^2} \;\approx\; \frac{(1+s^2+m^2)^2}{s^2}, \]

quartic in the mean. Centring and scaling (\(m = 0\), \(s = 1\)) give exactly 1.

PropositionC6 Nothing outside the policy's support is identifiable proved here

If every observed regressor lies in a subspace \(S\) and the noise is Gaussian, then for any \(v \perp S\) the parameters \(\beta\) and \(\beta + tv\) induce identical data distributions for every \(t\). The Fisher information along \(v\) is zero and no unbiased estimator of \(v^\top\beta\) has finite variance.

Proof. The likelihood depends on \(\beta\) only through \(\psi_t^\top\beta\), which is unchanged by adding \(tv\). Then Cramér–Rao. ∎

Predicted costs for rarely-taken candidates carry unidentified components, so steering toward them rests on extrapolation the data cannot support. This is what justifies C3's floor rather than treating it as a defect.

CorollaryC★ Convergence erodes the stability margin conditional on B2

By eigenvalue interlacing, adding the rank-one mean term raises the smallest eigenvalue at most to the second-smallest eigenvalue of the covariance:

\[ \gamma = 2\lambda_{\min}\big(\mathbb{E}[\psi\psi^\top]\big) \;\le\; 2\lambda_2\big(\operatorname{Cov}\psi\big) \;\le\; \frac{2\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(\psi)}{p-1}. \]

With C4, \(\gamma \to 0\) as fleet entropy vanishes, so \(g_{\mathrm{stab}} \to 0\): any fixed positive applied trust — including C3's floor — eventually exceeds the stability threshold.

This single corollary ties Parts B and C together: the better the fleet learns, the less it can afford to trust what it has learned. The predicted symptom is pred_gain decaying late in training while fit_gain holds.

Part D

Safety of the wrapper — the floor property, quantified

"Cannot drag the host below itself" is currently an implementation invariant. Part D gives it numbers and explains what trust learning actually responds to.

PropositionD1 PACT‑1's policy class nests the host's proved here

With trust off, \(g \equiv 0\) and \(\tilde\pi = \pi_{\text{host}}\) exactly; with learned trust, \(g \to 0\) as \(w \to -\infty\) and \(g_{\text{prev}} \to 0\). Hence \(\sup_{\text{PACT‑1}} J \ge \sup_{\text{host}} J\).

A statement about optimal policies, not about what training finds. It is gated bit-for-bit in the self-test; state it once and move on.

TheoremD2 How far steering can move the policy proved herechecked

For any predicted costs — including adversarial ones — \(K\) options and population-standard-deviation z-scores:

\[ \operatorname{range}(z) \le \sqrt{2K}, \qquad \mathrm{KL}\big(\pi_{\text{host}} \,\Vert\, \tilde\pi\big) \le \frac{g^2\kappa^2 K}{4}, \qquad \mathrm{TV} \le g\kappa\sqrt{K/8}, \]

and for single-step rewards in an interval of width \(R\): \(\big|\mathbb{E}_{\tilde\pi} r - \mathbb{E}_{\pi} r\big| \le R\, g\kappa\sqrt{K/8}\).

Proof. For the extreme pair, \((z_a - z_b)^2 \le 2(z_a^2 + z_b^2) \le 2\sum_k z_k^2 = 2K\). Write \(\tilde\pi_k = \pi_k e^{\delta_k}/\mathbb{E}_\pi e^{\delta}\) with \(\delta = -g\kappa z\); then \(\mathrm{KL}(\pi\Vert\tilde\pi) = \log\mathbb{E}_\pi e^{\delta} - \mathbb{E}_\pi\delta \le \operatorname{range}(\delta)^2/8\) by Hoeffding's lemma, and \(\operatorname{range}(\delta) \le g\kappa\sqrt{2K}\). Pinsker gives TV, and \(|\mathbb{E}_p f - \mathbb{E}_q f| \le \operatorname{range}(f)\,\mathrm{TV}\). ∎

TheoremD3 The trust gradient is a covariance test proved here

At the on-policy point, before clipping activates, with advantage \(A\) and entropy coefficient \(c_H\):

\[ \frac{\partial}{\partial w}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\tilde\pi}[A] + c_H\, H(\tilde\pi)\Big) = \kappa\, g'(w)\Big[-\operatorname{Cov}_{\tilde\pi}(A, z) + c_H\big(\operatorname{Cov}_{\tilde\pi}(z,\ell) - g\kappa\operatorname{Var}_{\tilde\pi}(z)\big)\Big], \]
\[ g'(w) = \mathrm{conf}\cdot(1-\eta)\cdot g_{\max}\,\sigma'(w + b). \]

Proof. With \(\tilde\ell = \ell - g\kappa z\), \(\partial\log\tilde\pi_k/\partial g = -\kappa(z_k - \bar z)\) where \(\bar z = \mathbb{E}_{\tilde\pi} z\). The reward term is \(\sum_k A_k\,\partial\tilde\pi_k/\partial g = -\kappa\operatorname{Cov}_{\tilde\pi}(A,z)\). For entropy, \(\partial H/\partial g = \kappa\operatorname{Cov}_{\tilde\pi}(z, \log\tilde\pi)\), and \(\log\tilde\pi = \ell - g\kappa z - \text{const}\). Batch advantage normalisation rescales but cannot flip the sign, because \(\mathbb{E}_{\tilde\pi}[\nabla\log\tilde\pi] = 0\). The EMA state and confidence are frozen in the implementation's recomputation, which gives \(g'(w)\). ∎

Three consequences. (a) Trust grows only when the estimator's ranking agrees with realised advantage strongly enough to beat the entropy term. (b) An uninformative estimator, \(\operatorname{Cov}(A,z) = 0\), receives zero reward-gradient; trust decays only through the entropy bonus, at a rate proportional to \(c_H\,g\,\kappa^2\operatorname{Var}(z)\) — slow with \(c_H = 0.01\). (c) The interior stationary trust is \(g^\circ = [\operatorname{Cov}(z,\ell) - \operatorname{Cov}(A,z)/c_H]/(\kappa\operatorname{Var} z)\): once host logits already encode the prediction, entropy regularisation retires the estimator.

Part E

Evaluation methodology as theory

The placebo regime is not only a credibility device. It identifies effects that a separate σ = 0 run cannot.

PropositionE1 Placebo days identify the effect of training under the dial proved under (a)–(c)(a), (b) to verify

Assume (a) the observation contains no rewards or travel times; (b) the arm's policy and any online estimator are frozen during test; (c) the harm is applied after simulation, which holds by construction. In the test cycle, days 4000–4099 — 49 with \(A \gt 0\) and 51 with \(A = 0\) exactly, since day 4000 opens the bump at zero:

(i) actions and simulator times do not depend on the weather;

(ii) on \(A = 0\) days, recorded time equals simulator time exactly;

(iii) the placebo-day difference between a σ‑trained and a σ0‑trained policy identifies the effect of training under the dial, uncontaminated by direct harm;

(iv) within a run, the wet-minus-placebo difference is the direct harm on the frozen policy, exactly \((m_i - 1)\,t_i^{\mathrm{sim}}\).

Proof. Under (a) and (b) each day's actions are a function of the previous day's actions only; under (c) the simulator never sees the dial. So the action and simulator-time process is identical on wet and dry days. On dry days \(g \equiv 1\) and the harm multiplier is exactly 1 (identity at zero), which gives (ii)–(iv). ∎

Verify

(a) Confirm in RouteRL 2.0.0 that previous_agents_plus_start_time is route counts plus departure time, with no travel times.

(b) Holds for arms evaluated deterministically. For PACT‑1 it is open: if its estimator keeps updating from harmed records during test, PACT‑1 is the only arm whose test actions respond to the weather. That is not a confound — the difference in wet-day degradation between PACT‑1 and the blind arm then measures the value of online identification, and it should be reported as such.

PropositionE2 The σ = 0 wrapper is the stock task proved by construction

At σ = 0 every reward and record passes through the wrapper unchanged, bit for bit, so the σ = 0 sweep is stock URB and every σ = 0 versus σ = 3 difference launched through the same entry point is attributable to the dial.
